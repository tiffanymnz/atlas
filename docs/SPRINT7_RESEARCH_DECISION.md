# Sprint 7 Research Decision

Date: September 16, 2026.

## Decision

The next Atlas prototype cluster is:

1. `division.shared_equally`
2. `fractions.numerator_denominator`
3. `percent.basics`

This is a prototype decision, not approval to publish production lessons. The research cards remain drafts until their adult-facing examples, bilingual language, memory anchors, and transfer checks are reviewed.

## Why this cluster is first

- Equal sharing establishes the equal-parts relationship used by fractions.
- Fraction meaning establishes the unit, magnitude, and equivalence needed to interpret percent as a rate per hundred.
- The GED Mathematical Reasoning assessment includes rational-number operations and real-world percent problems.
- The sequence supports connected representations instead of three isolated rules.
- Fraction instruction has a dedicated evidence-based practice guide, while several proposed algebra mnemonics still have weaker direct support.

## Evidence boundary

The [GED Mathematical Reasoning assessment guide](https://www.ged.com/content/dam/websites/ged/resources/en/assessment-guide-for-educators-math.pdf) prioritizes deeper conceptual understanding, procedural skill, and realistic application. It includes rational-number operations, multi-step percent problems, area and perimeter, equations, and inequalities.

The [IES fractions practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/15) supports building fraction understanding through coherent representations and magnitude rather than treating the numerator and denominator as disconnected whole numbers.

The [IES mathematics intervention guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/26) reports strong evidence for systematic instruction, precise mathematical language, concrete and semi-concrete representations, number lines, and deliberate word-problem instruction.

The [IES algebra practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/20) rates solved-problem analysis and use of algebraic structure as minimal evidence and intentional strategy selection as moderate evidence. For that reason, the balance-scale and inequality metaphors remain evidence-level C prototypes rather than production standards.

These IES guides primarily synthesize evidence from school-age learners. Applying them to adult GED learners is an informed adaptation, not direct proof. Atlas must test tone, examples, pacing, and anchor usefulness with adults before promotion.

## Existing hook corrections

The starter graph's short hooks remain source records, but they are not automatically approved teaching standards.

- “Bottom splits. Top counts.” is incomplete unless the learner first identifies the whole, understands equal-part size, and locates fraction magnitude.
- “Share fairly.” identifies a situation but does not distinguish finding group size from finding the number of groups.
- “Minimum” and “Maximum” are useful translations of inequality phrases, but learners still need to reason about the included boundary and number-line direction.
- “One side? Both sides.” protects equality only if learners understand the equal sign as a relationship rather than an instruction to calculate.

## Release gate

Before the selected cluster becomes production curriculum:

- close each selected card's unresolved question;
- review English and Spanish terminology for conceptual equivalence;
- test whether the model feels respectful and useful to adult learners;
- verify that recall prompts do not supply the answer;
- verify transfer with unfamiliar wording and realistic contexts;
- add complete English and Spanish lesson JSON;
- generate memory assets from the same source;
- extend automated completion, persistence, accessibility, and recovery tests.
