# Atlas Learning Standard

Atlas teaches meaning before memory. A mnemonic, visual, gesture, phrase, or pattern is useful only when it helps a learner reconstruct an understood concept. It must not replace the concept with a shortcut for passing a test.

## Required lesson sequence

Every Atlas lesson must make the following visible:

1. **Meaning** — what the concept is.
2. **Purpose** — what the concept helps a learner determine or explain.
3. **Model** — a visual, physical, verbal, or symbolic representation that makes the relationship concrete.
4. **Memory anchor** — a compact cue that helps reconstruct the meaning or procedure.
5. **Misconception contrast** — the tempting wrong interpretation and why it fails.
6. **Guided use** — supported application of the concept.
7. **Independent use** — application without the model supplying the answer.
8. **Recall** — retrieval after support is removed.
9. **Transfer** — recognition and application in unfamiliar wording or context.

## Memory-anchor requirements

A production memory anchor must:

- preserve the concept's actual meaning;
- explain what the concept does and when it applies;
- help reconstruct an answer instead of merely reciting one;
- connect to at least one model or representation;
- identify a likely misuse or boundary;
- work accurately in English and Spanish without forced literal wordplay;
- include a recall prompt and a transfer check;
- have an evidence level and a review status.

An arbitrary phrase that only encodes an answer is not sufficient. A useful shortcut may follow instruction, but it cannot substitute for instruction.

## Multiple representations

Atlas does not label learners as fixed visual, auditory, reading/writing, or kinesthetic types. Lessons should provide multiple accurate representations because different concepts and difficulties benefit from different supports. Learners may choose supports without being assigned a permanent category.

### Math model progression

For a concept that can be modeled with objects, design a connected path:

1. **Act:** the learner moves or groups objects; each object has a clear mathematical job.
2. **See:** a drawing or diagram preserves the same quantities and relationship. Connect each part of the drawing to the objects before removing the objects.
3. **Name and write:** introduce concise technical terms and symbols alongside the plain-language action. Explain the symbol from the model, including when that interpretation applies.
4. **Explain:** the learner says why the model and the expression show the same relationship. Feedback responds to the reasoning, not merely the selected answer.
5. **Use again:** vary the objects, arrangement, wording, and context; then ask for a solution without the original model. Fade supports only when the learner can explain and solve accurately.
6. **Retrieve later:** revisit the relationship after a delay and test transfer with technical exam wording.

Montessori Golden Beads and base-ten blocks are useful for quantity, place value, and exchanging. Other concepts may need number lines, equal-group arrays, area models, diagrams, or different representations. A learner's success with one attractive material is not evidence of transfer. Prefer a short performance check over a self-reported yes/no prerequisite check. Provide age-respectful contexts and test them with the intended learners.

## Research-card contract

Before a new concept becomes an Atlas production lesson, its research card must define:

- `concept_meaning`
- `concept_purpose`
- `memory_mechanism`
- `reconstruction_steps`
- `common_misuse`
- `visual_or_physical_anchor`
- `recall_prompt`
- `transfer_check`
- `bilingual_notes`
- `evidence_level`
- `review_status`

Ideas at evidence level C may be prototyped and tested. Unproven ideas must not be presented as established Atlas standards.

## Acceptance test

A learner has not demonstrated durable understanding merely by repeating the memory anchor. The lesson must separately test:

1. whether the learner can explain or identify the relationship;
2. whether the learner can use it without the anchor supplying the answer;
3. whether the learner can retrieve it after delay; and
4. whether the learner can transfer it to a new context.
