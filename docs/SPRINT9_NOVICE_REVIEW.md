# Sprint 9 novice review

## Decision

Lessons 008–009 are working prototypes, not production-approved curriculum. They remain marked `adult_review_required` until first-time adult learners can distinguish coverage from boundary distance and transfer that distinction without coaching.

## Human observation gate

Do not define area, perimeter, square unit, linear unit, boundary, or region for the learner during the attempt. Record hesitation and reasoning, not only selected answers.

After Lesson 008, ask the learner to:

1. explain what area measures without using the formula first;
2. explain why the answer uses square units;
3. rebuild the area of a different rectangle from rows and columns;
4. explain why adding all four sides does not answer a coverage question.

After Lesson 009, ask the learner to:

1. explain what perimeter measures without using a keyword shortcut;
2. trace an irregular boundary without omitting or repeating a side;
3. explain why the answer uses linear units;
4. compare two rectangles with equal area and different perimeters.

Revise immediately if a screen requires undeclared prior knowledge. Revise when two learners expose the same ambiguity, misleading visual cue, or vocabulary failure.

## Remaining questions

- Does the area grid communicate equal square units clearly on a small phone?
- Does the perimeter model look like a boundary path rather than an area diagram?
- Do learners understand that `length × width` counts squares instead of treating it as a magic rule?
- Can learners separate square-unit and linear-unit labels after the visuals are removed?
- Do the Spanish terms `región`, `borde`, `unidades cuadradas`, and `unidades lineales` sound natural to target learners?
