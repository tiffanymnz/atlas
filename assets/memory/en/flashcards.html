MATH-NS-COMP-0001 · comparison.more

How many more?

Learning goal: Understand that 'how many more' asks for the gap between two amounts.

Remember: Larger amount − smaller amount = difference

Recall: When a problem asks how many more one amount is than another, what should you find?

Answer: The gap between the amounts

MATH-NS-COMP-0002 · comparison.fewer

How many fewer?

Learning goal: Understand that 'how many fewer' asks for the same gap from the other direction.

Remember: Larger amount − smaller amount = how many fewer

Recall: Choose the rule that works for a how-many-fewer comparison.

Answer: Find the gap from larger to smaller

MATH-NS-COMP-0003 · difference

Difference

Learning goal: Understand that difference names the gap between two amounts.

Remember: Difference = the gap between two amounts

Recall: The difference between two amounts is...

Answer: the gap between them

MATH-NS-COMP-0004 · compare_unknown

Find the compared amount

Learning goal: Use a known larger amount and known difference to find the unknown smaller amount.

Remember: Known larger amount − known gap = missing smaller amount

Recall: You know the larger amount and the gap. What should you do?

Answer: Subtract the gap from the larger amount

MATH-NS-DIV-0005 · division.shared_equally

Share equally

Learning goal: Understand shared-equally division as finding the size of each equal group.

Remember: Total ÷ number of groups = amount in each group

Recall: When a total is shared equally and the number of groups is known, what does division find?

Answer: The amount in one group

MATH-NS-FRAC-0006 · fractions.numerator_denominator

Name equal parts

Learning goal: Understand a fraction by naming the whole, the equal-part size, and how many parts are present.

Remember: Denominator defines the part size; numerator counts copies of that part.

Recall: What must you know to explain the denominator and numerator of a fraction?

Answer: The whole, equal-part size, and number of parts counted

MATH-NS-PCT-0007 · percent.basics

Percent means per hundred

Learning goal: Understand percent as a rate per one hundred relative to a named whole.

Remember: Percent = how many per 100 of a named whole.

Recall: What two ideas must be identified before interpreting 37%?

Answer: The named whole and 37 parts per 100 of that whole

MATH-GEO-AREA-0008 · geometry.area

Measure the space inside

Learning goal: Understand area as the amount of flat space inside a boundary, measured with equal square units.

Remember: Area = equal square units covering the inside.

Recall: Why is area reported in square units?

Answer: Because equal squares cover a two-dimensional region

MATH-GEO-PERIM-0009 · geometry.perimeter

Measure the distance around

Learning goal: Understand perimeter as the total distance around a two-dimensional boundary, measured in linear units.

Remember: Perimeter = one complete trip around the outside.

Recall: Why is perimeter reported in ordinary units rather than square units?

Answer: Because it measures a one-dimensional distance along the boundary

MATH-ALG-INEQ-0010 · inequalities.at_least

Understand at least

Learning goal: Understand at least as an inclusive minimum and represent it with words, a number line, and a greater-than-or-equal-to inequality.

Remember: At least = the minimum counts, and more counts.

Recall: Why does “at least 25” use ≥ rather than >?

Answer: Because 25 itself is allowed, so equality must be included

MATH-ALG-INEQ-0011 · inequalities.no_more_than

Understand no more than

Learning goal: Understand no more than as an inclusive maximum and represent it with words, a number line, and a less-than-or-equal-to inequality.

Remember: No more than = the maximum counts, and less counts.

Recall: Why does “no more than 25” use ≤ rather than <?

Answer: Because 25 itself is allowed, so equality must be included
